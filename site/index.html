How to read this map

Grey, white and blank areas

Light grey: outside the selected area. Dark grey: inside the area but without values, for example cells where reference-period rainfall is too small for tercile categories, or (percent maps) below 10 mm. White on tercile maps: no category reaches 40%, or two categories tie. Sea cells have no CHIRPS data.

Smoothing and resolution. Maps are drawn on a 12× finer display grid with light Gaussian smoothing (σ = 0.6 grid cells); all numbers on this page use the original 0.25° grid. The model's own resolution is about 1°: each model cell covers 4×4 grid cells, so detail within a 1° box comes from local CHIRPS rainfall statistics used in the amount correction, not from the model.

Verification

Frozen forecasts compared with official CHIRPS v3.0 observations once a target period is complete. Each result is one season or month, so it shows what happened this time, not long-term reliability.

Historical performance

How forecasts made with the same method scored in past years against climatology (the observed frequency of each tercile in the training years, about one-third each). The main number is the cross-validated 1993–2016 score: each year is forecast with parameters fitted without that year. Later years are exploratory: they were seen while the method was chosen.

Rainfall climatology and domains

Where does a season's rain matter? Before a forecast domain is chosen, this review computes the observed climatology (seasonal total, annual total and the season's share), compares it with EMI's published long-term maps, and tests every combination of rainfall and annual-share cutoffs. Cutoffs are chosen for what the domain means climatologically, never to improve a forecast score.

Methods & data

In brief

Each cycle starts from the ECMWF SEAS5 seasonal ensemble issued on the 1st of the initialization month. For every 0.25° grid cell in Ethiopia the forecast rainfall is corrected toward the CHIRPS observed mean and variability, then turned into tercile probabilities (below, near or above normal relative to the cycle's CHIRPS reference period). The probabilities are finally blended with climatology using one weight per target, chosen to give the best past scores. Climatology assigns approximately one-third probability to each tercile; the calculations use the observed category frequencies of the training reference period.

Probabilities and amounts are produced separately: the probability skill (RPSS) on this site refers to the blended probabilities, and the rainfall amount skill (CRPSS) to the amount-corrected ensemble.

The raw benchmark is the uncorrected model: raw ECMWF members counted against each cell's CHIRPS tercile limits, with no correction, smoothing or blend. For May-initialized JJAS over 2017–2025 it scores RPSS −0.44, far below climatology, which is why calibration is needed.

CHIRPS v2.0 vs v3.0 (why and what changed, 11 October 2026)

CHIRPS v3.0 (Funk et al. 2026) replaced v2.0 for calibration and verification. Over Ethiopia, 1993–2025, v3.0 is about 15% wetter (consistent with its gauge-undercatch correction) while each season's share of annual rainfall and the ranking of years agree closely, so tercile limits move up. The same method was refitted on v3.0: the decision gates give the same outcome, and skill is similar overall. Each version is scored against its own observations.

Workflow (eight scripted stages)
  1. 1

    Download

    ECMWF SEAS5 (system 51) daily accumulated precipitation from the Copernicus CDS for the initialization month, 1993 to the forecast year.

    download_seasonal_forecasts_daily_c3s.py
  2. 2

    Inspect

    Inventory every year: initialization, lead times, member counts and accumulation increments (GRIB packing tolerance 0.2 mm).

    inspect_inputs.py
  3. 3

    Prepare

    De-accumulate to daily totals, sum the season and each month; CHIRPS on the same dates.

    prepare_seasonal.py · run_monthly.py
  4. 4

    Regrid

    Each 1° model cell is copied into its 4×4 block of 0.25° CHIRPS cells (no interpolation).

    regrid_seasonal.py
  5. 5

    Calibrate

    Per-cell amount correction, smoothed tercile counts and one climatology-blend weight per target.

    final_shared_blend.py
  6. 6

    Evaluate

    Nested cross-validation 1993–2016; exploratory fixed-fit scores for later years; whole-year significance tests.

    local_blend.py · decision_gates.py
  7. 7

    Deliver

    Maps, summaries and an offline gallery; forecasts frozen with SHA-256 hashes before observations are used.

    run_operational.py · build_season_products.py
  8. 8

    Verify

    Official CHIRPS v3.0 monthly files, checked against the archive where they overlap, scored per month and season.

    prepare_verification_2026.py · verify_frozen_2026.py

Every stage is a script in scripts/; documents in docs/ record each step, issue and decision (runbook: docs/38; per-season guides: docs/40).

Equations
  1. Amount correction per cell, equal weight per year: x̂ = max(0, μobs + r·(x − μmodel)), r = clip(σobs/σmodel, 0.5, 2)
  2. Tercile probabilities from the corrected members against CHIRPS tercile limits, smoothed: (n + 0.5)/(M + 1.5)
  3. Climatology blend: pfinal = (1 − λ)·pmodel + λ·pclimatology, with one λ per target fitted on leave-one-year-out RPS.
    pclimatology is, for each grid cell, the observed frequency of each tercile in the training reference years (about one-third each; it differs slightly because of sample size, category limits and ties).
  4. Skill score: RPSS = 1 − RPSforecast / RPSclimatology; RPSS +0.05 means a 5% lower ranked probability score than climatology. CRPSS is the same for rainfall amounts.
Method and references

Rainfall amounts are corrected cell by cell with mean–variance scaling (also called simple bias adjustment or variance scaling; Teutschbein & Seibert 2012; Torralba et al. 2017; Manzanas et al. 2019): each member is shifted to the CHIRPS mean and its anomaly rescaled by the ratio of CHIRPS to model standard deviation (equations above). It corrects the mean and spread, not the shape of the distribution; it is not quantile mapping. The model mean and spread are pooled over all members and years, with equal weight per year (25 members to 2016, 51 from 2017); training years 1993–2025.

Probabilities are the share of corrected members in each CHIRPS tercile (ensemble relative frequency; Wilks 2019), smoothed so no category is 0 or 1, then blended with climatology. Weighting a model against climatology follows Rajagopalan et al. (2002) and Robertson et al. (2004); here one weight per target is fitted by minimising the ranked probability score of leave-one-year-out probabilities.

Scores: ranked probability score (Epstein 1969) and continuous ranked probability score (Hersbach 2000), as skill scores against climatology. Forecasts: ECMWF SEAS5 (Johnson et al. 2019); observations: CHIRPS v3.0 (Funk et al. 2026), v2.0 before 11 October 2026 (Funk et al. 2015).

Choices of this project, not taken from a single paper: the 0.5–2 limits on the spread ratio, mean-only correction where the model spread is below 1 mm, clipping at 0 mm (its weakness in dry months, issue I-08), equal weighting of years, the count smoothing and one blend weight shared by all cells. A square-root-space amount correction passed the gate for amounts and is recommended for the next cycle (docs/36). Details: docs/46. DOIs checked on Crossref, 11 October 2026.

How the method was chosen

A change is adopted only if it improves the cross-validated 1993–2016 scores (Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 over the five JJAS targets) and does not worsen 2017–2025. Also evaluated and not adopted: regularized Dirichlet recalibration and per-cell or regime-specific blend weights.

Calibration parameters are fitted mostly on 25-member hindcasts and applied to 51-member forecasts. In 2017–2025 the 51-member forecasts scored about the same as, or slightly better than, 25-member subsets, so the transfer did not degrade scores in the years tested; this does not show it is neutral in every year or region. The corrected ensemble is under-dispersive (spread smaller than error), which the climatology blend partly offsets. Monthly and seasonal outlooks are calibrated separately: for JJAS 2026 over all Ethiopia the corrected monthly means sum to 432 mm against 430 mm for the season (+1.7 mm).

Quality control
  • Every forecast file is inventoried; re-downloaded years were bit-identical, confirming SEAS5 system 51.
  • Small negative daily increments (≤ 0.15 mm) are GRIB packing rounding; tolerated up to 0.2 mm and clipped, and the seasonal sum is checked.
  • CHIRPS sea cells (223) are always missing; no partial season enters any fit.
  • Verification observations are compared with the archive in the overlap year (tolerance 0.0001 mm/day) before use.
Limitations
  • Few evaluation years and single verification seasons give wide uncertainty; regional and monthly results are noisy.
  • The later evaluation years were inspected during method development; the cleanest evidence is the cross-validated 1993–2016 score.
  • The 0.25° maps repeat 1° model information; they add no dynamical detail.
  • All cycles are research reconstructions produced after their initialization dates, not official EMI or ICPAC forecasts.
