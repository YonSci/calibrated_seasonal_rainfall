## Purpose

Produce clearer Ethiopia maps from the completed final shared-blend forecasts for JJAS, June, July, August and September 2026. This is a plotting and derived-field stage. It does not refit calibration or change rainfall, tercile thresholds, probabilities, regridding, or masks.

The existing `blend_probability` already contains a **tercile probability forecast**. This update adds a single dominant-tercile map, using category-specific probability colors similar to the supplied ICPAC example, and red–white–green anomaly maps. These are your research products, not official ICPAC or EMI forecasts. No agency logos are added.

## 5. Dominant-tercile calculation

For each eligible grid cell, take the three probabilities directly from `blend_probability`, in the order below, near, above.

Example: `[0.60, 0.25, 0.15]` means 60% below normal, 25% near normal and 15% above normal. The map uses the below-normal palette at 60%. This does not mean rainfall will be 60% below normal.

- Below normal: yellow → orange → red.
- Near normal: pale cyan → darker cyan.
- Above normal: pale green → dark green.
- White: maximum probability is below the display threshold, or the leading categories are tied.
- Gray: inside the region but not eligible for a probability forecast.
- Outside the region: blank.

The default display threshold is 40%. This is an explicitly chosen communication setting, not an ICPAC standard, statistical significance cutoff, or calibration parameter. A 39%/31%/30% forecast still exists in the NetCDF; the map simply labels its leading signal weak. Near-normal and weak signal are different concepts: near-normal must actually be the leading category and pass the same threshold.

A tie within 1e-8 is unresolved rather than assigned arbitrarily to the first category. The NetCDF stores both `dominant_tercile` (no probability cutoff) and `display_tercile` (cutoff applied). Codes: -2 outside/ineligible, -1 unresolved, 0 below, 1 near, 2 above. In the raw dominant field unresolved means tied; in the display field it also includes weak maxima.

To show all unique leading categories, including weak ones:

```bat
python scripts\plot_forecast_products.py --min-probability 0.3333333333333333 --regenerate
```

Do not increase probabilities to make the colors resemble another agency's map. The final shared blend intentionally shrinks probabilities toward climatology.

## 6. Anomalies

Let F be the corrected 51-member ensemble mean and C the CHIRPS mean for the same target period over 1993–2025.

```text
anomaly_mm = F - C
anomaly_percent = 100 * (F - C) / C
```

Example: F=400 mm and C=500 mm gives -100 mm and -20%. This differs from a 60% probability of below-normal rainfall: one describes an amount difference, the other the probability of a category.

The percentage plot masks cells where C is less than 10 mm, avoiding unstable percentages in very dry locations. This display safeguard is not a new climatological dry-season definition. The mm map remains available there if the cell is amount-eligible.

Default scales:

- JJAS: -300 to +300 mm.
- Every individual month: -100 to +100 mm.
- Percent anomaly: -100% to +100%.

White spans ±2% of the color-scale limit: ±6 mm for JJAS, ±2 mm for months, ±2 percentage points for the percentage plot. White does not mean a statistically defined near-normal tercile. Beyond-scale values retain their exact numerical values in NetCDF; arrows on the colorbar indicate saturated colors and the report counts those cells.

Example scale adjustment:

```bat
python scripts\plot_forecast_products.py --jjas-anomaly-limit-mm 400 --monthly-anomaly-limit-mm 150 --percent-anomaly-limit 150 --regenerate
```

Do not reuse the supplied ICPAC weekly map's ±60 scale for a four-month total without assessing its units and range. Its period is 29 September–6 October 2026.

## 7. Where masking belongs

The established workflow remains: prepare totals → regrid to the common grid → align/apply intended masks and eligibility rules → fit/evaluate or summarize → display.

Your final report explicitly records **no separate land–ocean mask**. The Ethiopia `region_mask` controls display and regional summaries; the current calibration fit uses eligible cells across the common domain. CHIRPS eligibility, an Ethiopia boundary, and a physical land–ocean mask are different things.

These new plots use `region_mask AND probability_eligible` for probabilities and `region_mask AND amount_eligible` for amounts. Adding a shapefile outline does not change either. Gray here means ineligible; it must not be described as ICPAC's climatologically dry-season mask. No new dry-season or physical land mask is invented.

## 8. Interpretation limits

- The shared probability blend does not modify the continuous corrected ensemble. Therefore the amount anomaly and the calibrated dominant category are related but need not correspond exactly at every cell.
- Do not classify the ensemble-mean rainfall against q1/q2 as a substitute for the highest-probability tercile.
- Monthly calibration is separate from JJAS calibration; corrected monthly means need not add to the corrected JJAS mean, and monthly probabilities must not be added or averaged to obtain JJAS probabilities.
- The archive ends in 2025 for observations. These 2026 maps are reconstructed forecasts, not a 2026 skill evaluation.
- The uploaded report gives clipping fractions of about 22–24% for June–August across all eligible common-domain member/cell values. These are not Ethiopia land-area percentages. Before changing the amount correction, inspect where clipping occurs; it can affect means and percent anomalies in dry areas. This plotting update does not alter the validated correction.
